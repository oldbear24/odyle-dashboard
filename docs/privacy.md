# Privacy and persistence

Odyle is an unofficial fan project run by **oldbear24**. Questions or requests about your data: open an issue at [github.com/oldbear24/odyle-dashboard/issues](https://github.com/oldbear24/odyle-dashboard/issues). For anything you would rather not post in public, a security problem included, report it privately on GitHub under the repository's **Security** tab (**Report a vulnerability**).

Discord authenticates the application account. PocketBase stores the provider identity mapping and account email/display name needed for sign-in, plus memberships and each character's region/server, name, Odyle total and observation timestamps. The room API exposes display names and opted-in character data, not account emails, authentication secrets or Discord tokens. The email is used for one thing only: telling you when your account signs in from a new device. Nobody else sees it.

The membership answer (whether your game account has the membership: yes, no or not sure) is kept with the account until you change it or delete the account, and is in the export. Rooms never see the answer; they see the natural pool's cap it gives the characters you share with them (840 or 560), which the Odyle clock uses. It changes no value read from the game.

Notification settings (which notifications, where, reminder times, cap alerts, and quiet hours with the time zone they are in) are kept with the account until you change them or delete it; with cap alerts on, each character also keeps whether you were last told its natural pool is nearly full or full, which nobody else sees. Characters belong to accounts. Leaving, closing a room or signing out does not erase stored values. Each change of a character's Odyle is also kept for 30 days as its history, with the name of the packet adapter that decoded it and, from newer Windows apps, the game's two numbers (natural pool and items) and whether the change was a regeneration tick, a spend or an item, visible to the owner and to rooms the character is shared in, and deleted after that.

Your data, your choice: **Account & data** downloads everything stored for your account as one JSON file (account, sign-in identity, notification settings, characters and their history, rooms, wishlist entries, planned runs, tasks, Windows app sessions, paired Windows apps and the invite links you made), and deletes single characters or the whole account. Deleting the account removes its characters, history, memberships, wishlist entries and tasks; rooms it owned pass to their longest-standing player, or close. The server records when the account last used Odyle (at most hourly). Operators may set a period after which unused accounts are deleted automatically; by default accounts are kept until their owner deletes them. **Sign out everywhere** ends every other browser sign-in and every paired Windows app; **Remove PC** ends one paired Windows app and leaves the rest signed in. Sharing is chosen per character and room; turning it off immediately hides that character from other room members while keeping it in the owner's account. Last saved values may be stale or offline; they remain visible with their timestamp where sharing is enabled, but are excluded from fresh readiness.

The app detects its own character's name, server and region from game packets and saves those normalized identity fields to the paired account; neither the app nor the dashboard offers to type them. This creates an unknown reading, not a confirmed zero; it cannot set Odyle, ownership, observation timestamps or reading confidence. Repeating a saved identity preserves its existing history.

Character pictures: the backend sends each saved character's name, server and region to PLAYNC's public AION 2 character search and downloads the official portrait it lists, about once a day per character while it is played. A character the search does not list is asked for less and less often, down to once a week, and a character no reader has seen for 60 days is not sent again until a reader sees it. Browsers load pictures only from the Odyle backend, never from NCSOFT. Players cannot upload or change pictures. The same search answer gives the character's official class, level and PLAYNC character id, kept on the character so cards show its class and open its armory profile; nothing else is asked for it. Picture files are served from unguessable links without sign-in, like most chat avatars; the backend hands those links only to the owner and to rooms the character is shared in, but anyone who is given a link can open it.

Armory: anyone, signed in or not, can look up an AION 2 Global character's gear, combat power, item level and stats as PLAYNC's official character pages publish them. The backend asks PLAYNC for the character a reader opens, sending the character's server and PLAYNC character id (and, for a search, the name typed), and keeps a copy of what it shows: name, level, class, race, server, title, gear, stats and Daevanion boards. It keeps no images, skills or rankings. Kept profiles are public to everyone who opens them, like PLAYNC's own pages. A profile nobody opened for 30 days is deleted. A character PLAYNC no longer lists loses its profile at the next lookup. To keep a character out of the armory, ask the operator, who marks its profile hidden (it is then never shown or fetched again). The armory saves nothing about who looks: no account, no search history on the server. The browser remembers the dashboard version it last showed (`odyle.seenVersion`, for the note after an update), the region and server last searched (`odyle.armory`) and the characters you bookmark, with the numbers their profile showed when you last opened it (`odyle.armory.bookmarks`, up to 50). Bookmarks never leave the browser; clearing its site data removes them. Rate limits count requests per address in server memory only.

Windows app downloads and updates: the backend asks GitHub's public API for the newest release of the Windows app at most every ten minutes and passes the version and package links to browsers; no account data is involved and browsers do not contact GitHub until a download link is clicked. An installed Windows app contacts GitHub itself (`api.github.com`, `github.com`) when it starts and about once a day to look for and download new releases, sending only a standard request that names its version.

Manual totals are rejected. The Windows reader follows the local AION2 process TCP connections using the OS connection table and reads packets through Npcap. It does not read game memory or modify packets/gameplay. Raw traffic remains in local bounded memory and is neither written to disk nor sent to PocketBase. Decoded totals are published under the adapter name `global-reference-v1` (`global-reference-v2` from the release with the resource-id decoder); the backend stores readings only from adapters it lists. Operators can pause every packet reader from the server: paired apps then stop capturing and close Npcap until the pause ends. Historical manual values remain stored but invalid for current readiness. Confirmed zero is a real value; unknown is displayed as a dash. A heartbeat updates presence without reconfirming energy. Session rotation and character switching invalidate current confidence without erasing last saved totals.

The dashboard keeps its sign-in token in the browser's localStorage (`odyle.account`), together with the last opened page (`odyle.location`), the chosen language (`odyle.language`), whether a room page shows only live characters (`odyle.liveOnly`), the checklist list last shown under Daily & weekly (`odyle.taskScope`), the text of the last announcement you closed, so it stays hidden until a new one is posted (`odyle.announcement.hidden`), and, on the room owner's browser, the invite codes it created until they expire, a week at most (`odyle.invites`, with each link's id, role and expiry; the server keeps only their digests; signing out of the browser clears them), and the desktop pairing code it asked for until the app uses it or it expires (`odyle.pairing`, with the account id and expiry, so the pairing dialog shows the same link again; the server keeps only its digest; signing out of the browser clears it). Invite links carry the code in the URL fragment, which browsers do not send to servers. Every tab of that browser shares the sign-in and it survives closing the browser; it ends when you choose **Sign out of this browser**, which clears the sign-in and the last page, when you **Sign out everywhere** from another browser, or after seven days without opening the dashboard. On a shared computer, sign out when you are done. Windows credentials are protected by DPAPI for the current OS user. The five-minute one-use desktop code pairs the app to its owner's account; do not share it with another person. A paired app keeps a device key that lets it sign in again without a new link; the server stores only its digest with the PC's Windows computer name (so **Account & data** can list it) and the pairing and last sign-in times, and deletes it when the app disconnects, the account signs out everywhere or is deleted (an account keeps its 10 most recently used pairings). Room invitations are separate and never grant another account ownership of your characters.

Room invitations: for each invite link the server keeps the digest of its code, the room, the role it gives, which account made it, the owner's optional label, when it expires (after 30 minutes, 24 hours or 7 days), its use limit and how many joined with it. The room's owner sees these details from any browser and can revoke each link; the expiry job deletes a link three days after it expired, and **Account & data** exports the links you made. A link whose maker deletes the account forgets the maker. The link names the room's current owner as the inviter, so after the maker hands the room over (or leaves it) the new owner is named, not the maker; the maker's export still lists the link while it is kept. Opening an invite link asks the server what it opens, sending the code in the request body, never in the address, so it reaches no server or proxy log; this preview is open without an account and limited per client address. While the link still works, whoever holds it sees the room name, the display name of the room's owner, the role and the number of members, which they would see on joining anyway, and no member names, characters or Odyle. An expired or used-up link shows the room name only, and any other code shows nothing. Links shared in Discord and other apps unfurl with the same general title, text and app icon for every page; they never name the room.

Calendar feed: if you make one under **Account & data**, the dashboard keeps a fingerprint of its private link, never the link itself. Whoever has the link (your calendar app, or anyone you give it to) sees the dungeon, room and time of the runs you are going to, never notes. Making a new link or stopping the feed ends the old one at once.

Slot machine: the dashboard keeps how many spins your account has and which cosmetic items it won and wears; members of your rooms see the items you wear next to your name. Your data download lists them, and deleting the account deletes them. Whether the slot machine plays its sounds is kept in this browser only (`odyle.slotSound`).

Discord run reminders: a room's posts show the runs' dungeon, time, note and the display names of those going. Only if you turn on **A mention in Discord** in your notifications does a post of a run you are going to mention you, by the Discord account you sign in with, so Discord notifies you; that Discord id goes to Discord in the post and nowhere else. The server keeps each post's Discord message id for two days so it can edit the post after a change. When the room's owner turns on readiness, the posts also count how many of those going can claim a cube (for example 4/5), never which characters or their Odyle.

Run reminders and cap alerts follow your account's **Notifications** (see above). A browser shows them while the dashboard is open there once you allow notifications (the runs already announced stay in that browser's `odyle.reminded`), and the dashboard fetches only the runs you are going to in the next 24 hours from the Odyle backend. **Push** is a choice of each device: turned on, the browser subscribes at its own push service (Google for Chrome, Edge and Android browsers, Mozilla for Firefox, Apple for Safari, Microsoft for Edge on Windows) and the backend keeps that subscription (the push service's address for the device, its encryption keys, the language and a device name like *Edge on Windows*) until you turn push off there, the push service forgets it or you delete the account. Each message (a run reminder or cap alert: dungeon, room, note, character name and Odyle numbers) is encrypted for that device alone before it leaves the backend, so the push service only passes on what it cannot read; it does see that a message went to the device, and when. Your data download lists the devices. A room owner may connect a Discord channel webhook under **Room settings**: the backend then posts each planned run there (dungeon, start time, note and the display names of the members going) 15 minutes before it starts. The webhook URL is stored hidden and never returned to any client; whoever reads that Discord channel sees the posts, so connect only a channel your party uses. **Add to calendar** builds an iCalendar file in the browser; nothing about it is sent anywhere.

Offline files: the dashboard's service worker keeps the page and its built scripts, styles, fonts and icons in the browser's cache so it opens without a connection. It never stores API answers, readings or account data.

Operators: the PocketBase dashboard's Odyle page shows superusers which accounts have a Windows app connected and the requests those apps send (account name, character identity and value, session ids, timings and rejection reasons; never tokens, codes or game traffic). The newest 200 requests are kept in server memory only, are not written to the database and disappear on restart. So is what each app's heartbeat says about its reader (waiting, listening, reading, a problem and the like) and the game build it reads, kept for two minutes after the app's last contact to warn the operator when a game patch breaks reading. Its server status page counts the last hour's requests per minute and route, with their handling times and rejection reasons but without accounts, also in memory only, together with how many of them came from private network addresses (no address is kept), and shows totals of what the database holds. It also shows activation and feature-use totals: how many accounts paired a PC, have a reading or share a character in a room, how many rooms read together or use runs, wishlists and Discord reminders, how many accounts keep tasks, and the Windows app versions in use. One row of these totals is stored each night so trends survive restarts; they count accounts and rooms but never name, list or follow one, and only superusers see them. The same data is already stored or derivable in the database superusers administer.

Request/IP logs and analytics are disabled by default. The dashboard loads nothing from other sites: its fonts and icons come with it, and its content security policy lets the browser reach only the Odyle server, so no font service or tracker sees your address.

Backups: the server backs up its whole database every night (accounts with their Discord sign-in identity and email, characters and their history, rooms, wishlists, runs, tasks, paired PCs, settings and portraits) and keeps the newest seven, on the server's own disk or, when the operator sets it up, in private S3-compatible storage. Deleting a character or an account removes it from the live database at once, but it stays in the backups made before, so for up to seven days by default; restoring a backup brings back the data as it was then. Only the operators can open the backups. Provider client secrets stay on the backend; never include them in browser environment variables or source control.

Characters nobody plays: a character that no Windows reader has read or seen for 12 months is deleted with its Odyle history by a nightly job, and its rooms stop showing it. Playing it again with the reader saves it anew; the game names it as before.
